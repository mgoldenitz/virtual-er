# Virtual ER waiting evidence and Ontario ER wait figures

Researched 2026-11-24 by the swarm Researcher. Checked by Claude the same day: 22 of 23 links opened (HTTP 200) and the quoted figure was found on each page or in each PDF; the PubMed record (blocked to scripts) was confirmed through the NCBI API.

For the README and slides only. No figure goes into the app (SPEC rule 8).

## Headline findings

- Virtual waiting evidence is thin. One real Canadian deployment (Sault Area Hospital, Ontario). All its figures are hospital-reported or from news. No peer-reviewed outcome evaluation and no published safety-event data. The peer-reviewed items are patient-perception studies.
- LWBS = left without being seen. The Sault Area Hospital LWBS numbers differ between sources because they compare different groups (see conflicts).
- Credit Valley Hospital (Trillium Health Partners) is slower than average: Ontario Health's July 2026 average time to first assessment is 2.7 h, against 2.0 h provincially (owner's ER Wait Time Scorecard, see Part B).
- Safest wording: "hospital-reported pilot results from one Ontario hospital; no independent evaluation yet". Do not claim safety.

## Part A: virtual or off-site ER waiting

1. Virtual emergency waiting room has positive results at northern Ontario hospital. CBC News (Alex Flood), 22 Dec 2025. https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/9.7023124 (full page: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/sudbury/sault-area-hospital-virtual-waiting-room-emergency-department-9.7023124). Opened and confirmed.
   - Aug to Nov 2025 pilot (350 patients): low-acuity wait for physician assessment fell from 5.8 h to 2.7 h; LWBS fell from 10% to 5.3%.
   - Quote: "For "low acuity" patients, physician initial assessment decreased from 5.8 hours to 2.7 hours, while the length of stay was reduced from 7.7 hours to 4 hours. There was also a drop in the number of patients who left the hospital without being seen, from 10 per cent to 5.3 per cent." Also: "between 87 and 89 per cent of users were satisfied, while more than 90 per cent indicated they would use the virtual waiting room again."
   - Eligible complaints: cough or sore throat, earache, rash, dental pain, prescription renewal, suture or staple removal, minor cuts and scrapes, sprains and strains, burning with urination.

2. Virtual waiting room here to stay at Sault Area Hospital. SooToday (Greg McGrath-Goudie), 16 Dec 2025. https://www.sootoday.com/local-news/virtual-waiting-room-here-to-stay-at-sault-area-hospital-11633266 . Opened and confirmed.
   - States the pilot figures as 90th-percentile values, from a hospital staff report to the board.
   - Quote: "The 90th percentile wait time for initial physician assessment improved from 5.8 hours to 2.7 hours, and from 7.7 hours to 4.0 hours for total length of stay under the project."

3. Health minister mulls Ontario pilot that sees some ER patients wait at home. CBC News (Bobbi-Jean MacKinnon), 14 Jan 2026. https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/new-brunswick/new-brunswick-emergency-room-wait-home-text-pilot-ontario-9.7038870 . Opened and confirmed.
   - The 25% wait cut excludes time spent waiting at home. With that time included it is about 22%.
   - Quote: "These figures do not include the time the 350 pilot participants spent waiting at home... With that time included, the decrease was closer to 22 per cent, with 90 per cent of the low-acuity patients waiting a maximum of about six hours to be seen by a doctor and seven and a half hours to be discharged."
   - Limit: Dr. Fraser Mackay says letting patients wait at home "does nothing for the root cause issue of the downstream overcapacity."
   - The pilot expanded "to accommodate up to 21 patients daily, up from 10."

4. Ontario hospital tries virtual waiting room to address wait times, overcrowding in ER. The Canadian Press (Sharif Hassan), via CP24, 10 Mar 2026. https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/03/10/ontario-hospital-tries-virtual-waiting-room-to-address-wait-times-overcrowding-in-er/ . Opened and confirmed. Syndicated copy: https://www.canhealth.com/2026/03/18/virtual-ed-helps-reduce-waits-for-some-patients/ (18 Mar 2026).
   - Time in the ER "has also dropped by half for patients using the tool."
   - Cost: "the annual subscription cost for the product is $40,000 annually" (canhealth copy).
   - Caution: Dr. Iris Gorfinkel urges independent research. These are comparisons with "patients who wait in the hospital with similar symptoms", not a controlled study.

5. HSN virtual emergency department pilot ended in 2021 despite glowing reviews. Sudbury.com (Tyler Clarke), 10 Apr 2026, syndicated on Elliot Lake Today. https://www.elliotlaketoday.com/local-news/hsn-virtual-emergency-department-pilot-ended-in-2021-despite-glowing-reviews-12127356 . Opened and confirmed.
   - Newest Sault Area Hospital data: about 1,000 patients since August 2025.
   - Quote: "The 90th percentile of patients... who used the virtual home waiting room took 3.1 hours for a physician initial assessment compared to 6.5 hours among those who went straight to the emergency department. ... length of stay among virtual home waiting room patients was 4.9 hours compared to 10.2 hours... 5.6 per cent of virtual home waiting room patients left without being seen compared to 13 per cent of those who arrived directly."
   - Caveat: users vs non-users, so selection bias is likely. Figures from a hospital spokesperson.

6. Virtual Home Waiting Room. Sault Area Hospital (hospital page, undated). https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/ . Opened and confirmed.
   - Design reference: online registration, then staff check eligibility; texts about hourly give queue position, then a come-in text; 2.5 h to respond or the patient is removed; age over 3 months and an SMS-capable phone; runs 9 a.m. to 9 p.m.; queue time counts toward total ED wait.
   - Safety wording: "please do not continue using this service" if you are uncertain how serious your condition is; if it worsens, "head directly to the nearest Emergency Department or call 911 immediately."

7. "Eliminating the waiting game": patient interview perspectives of a proposed online emergency department queuing tool to reduce in-hospital wait times. Godinho, Dalmia, Phadke, Lalani, Wegier (Humber River Health). CJEM 28(5):417-430, May 2026. DOI 10.1007/s43678-026-01140-3. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41920515/ . Abstract only.
   - 28 adults interviewed. "Most participants reported they would have used the tool for their last ED visit or on behalf of family and friends, felt it was secure, and were comfortable waiting at home for an average four hours."
   - Patients asked for "regular symptom check-ins to provide updates and self-management strategies while waiting at home." Perception data, not outcome data.

8. Use of a Semiautomatic Text Message System to Improve Satisfaction With Wait Time in the Adult Emergency Department. Ehrler et al. (Geneva). JMIR Medical Informatics, 2022. DOI 10.2196/34488. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/ . Abstract only.
   - "97 of 100 (97%) patients were satisfied, with most patients waiting outside the ED but inside the hospital." Nurse adoption was slow "because of the perceived additional workload associated with its use." No LWBS or safety outcomes.

9. Factors Associated with Patients Leaving Without Being Seen in a Canadian Emergency Department. Odorizzi et al. (The Ottawa Hospital). Western Journal of Emergency Medicine, 2025. DOI 10.5811/westjem.47302. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12815564/ . Abstract only.
   - Supports the CTAS 4/5 target group: "CTAS 4 patients had 134% higher adjusted odds, and CTAS 5 patients (lowest acuity) had 176% higher adjusted odds of leaving" (vs CTAS 2). "Of 170,536 ED visits, 15,473 (9.1%) patients left without being seen." Single site.

10. Roughly 500,000 Canadians left ERs before seeing a doctor last year, data shows. CBC Marketplace, 21 Nov 2025. https://www.cbc.ca/news/marketplace/hospital-wait-times-9.6983849 . Opened and confirmed.
    - "Ontario had the lowest percentage, at roughly five per cent." A physician says patients who leave come back sicker (anecdote, not a measured rate).

11. Designs, facilitators, barriers, and lessons learned during the implementation of emergency department led virtual urgent care programs in Ontario, Canada. Hall et al. Frontiers in Digital Health, 2022. DOI 10.3389/fdgth.2022.946734. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.946734/full . Abstract only.
    - Adjacent, not a waiting room: 14 Ontario virtual urgent care pilots aimed at "ED diversion of patients with low acuity issues". Barriers: behaviour change, IT, privacy and risk, data collection.

12. Low-Acuity Patients Delay High-Acuity Patients in EDs. Luo, Bayati, Plambeck, Aratow (Stanford). Working paper, about January 2021. https://web.stanford.edu/~bayati/papers/externality.pdf . Opened and confirmed (abstract and introduction).
    - "the arrival of an additional low-acuity patient substantially increases the wait time to start of treatment for high-acuity patients". US data; label it a working paper.

### Conflicts and what is newer (Part A)

- LWBS at Sault Area Hospital: 10% to 5.3% (CBC, Dec 2025, before/after) vs 5.6% virtual vs 13% direct (Sudbury.com, Apr 2026, users vs non-users). Different comparisons, not a contradiction. Use the newer one only with that label.
- Wait reduction: 25% excludes home waiting time; about 22% with it (CBC, 14 Jan 2026).
- Metric: CBC gives no percentile; SooToday says 90th percentile. Say 90th percentile only when citing SooToday.
- Safety: no data on adverse events or deterioration at home from any source. State that as a gap.

## Part B: Ontario ER wait figures

Source of the July 2026 figures in items 1 to 3: the owner's ER Wait Time Scorecard (Power BI), built from Ontario Health "Time Spent in Emergency Departments" monthly data, July 2025 to July 2026, exported September 2026 (data: NACRS, CIHI). These figures replace the January 2026 figures as the newest. The scorecard's January 2026 values are the same as the Researcher's January 2026 values.

1. Provincial average time to first assessment: 2.0 hours, July 2026 (1.7 hours in January 2026). Ontario Health, "Time Spent in Emergency Departments". https://ontariohealth.ca/system/reporting/performance/time-spent-in-emergency-departments . Opened and confirmed.
   - January 2026 page text: "On average, patients waited 1.7 Hours", measured "from checking in at triage or registration, to their first assessment by a doctor, nurse-practitioner or dentist." Source: NACRS, CIHI. Averages only.

2. Credit Valley Hospital, Trillium Health Partners: 2.7 hours average to first assessment, July 2026 (2.2 hours in January 2026). Same data.
   - July 2026: Ontario 2.0, Trillium-Mississauga 1.5, Trillium-Queensway 1.1. January 2026: Ontario 1.7, Mississauga 1.4, Queensway 1.2.
   - Credit Valley was above Ontario in all 13 months (2.1 to 2.7 h). Queensway is an urgent care centre, not a full ED.
   - Admitted patients, July 2026 (scorecard): Credit Valley 26.0 h average time in the ED, against 17.7 h for Ontario (rank 147 of 154); 15.4% left the ED within the 8-hour target, against 28.2% for Ontario (goal 90%).
   - Auditor General of Ontario, Dec 2023 (https://auditor.on.ca/en/content/annualreports/arreports/en23/AR_emergencydepts_en23.pdf): Credit Valley site 2.3 hours, 2022/23.
   - MEI annex (https://www.iedm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ANNEX_1_2025-26_ER_Wait_Times_Final.pdf, April 2025 to Feb 2026): Credit Valley median time to physician initial assessment 1:55; median ED length of stay 5:03.
   - No CTAS 4/5-specific figure for Credit Valley was found.

3. Low-urgency non-admitted patients, July 2026: Ontario average length of stay 3.2 hours, 72.9% within the 4-hour target (January 2026: 3.0 hours, 76.6%). Same data.
   - July 2026 by site: Credit Valley 3.9 h, 60.9% within target; Mississauga 3.4 h, 69.7%; Queensway 2.0 h, 95.1%.
   - The scorecard defines low urgency as CTAS 4 to 5 (4-hour target). The Ontario Health page says only "e.g. sore throat or vomiting".

4. 90th percentile time to physician initial assessment: 2.7 h in 2020-21, 4.5 h in 2024-25. CCPA (Andrew Longhurst), "Failure, by design", 11 May 2026. https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/failure-by-design-ontarios-deepening-hospital-funding-crisis/ . Opened and confirmed. Also CP24, 11 May 2026: https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/05/11/under-immense-strain-wait-times-at-ontario-emergency-rooms-have-spiked-over-past-5-years-study-says/
   - CCPA is a policy think tank citing CIHI; the CIHI table was not opened.
   - Provincial targets (Ontario Health operational direction, https://ontariohealth.ca/news/operational-direction-priorities-spring-summer-24-25): 90th percentile time to physician initial assessment 3.4 hours or less; 90th percentile ED length of stay for non-admitted patients 6 hours or less.

5. All-patient average 118 minutes and 90th percentile 257 minutes, 2022/23; CTAS 4 and 5 were about 23% of ED visits. Auditor General of Ontario, Dec 2023 (link above). Opened and confirmed.
   - "lower-acuity patients, specifically those assigned a CTAS 4 (less urgent) or CTAS 5 (non-urgent)... accounted for about 23% (or 1.29 million) of all emergency department visits in 2022/23."
   - Average LWBS rate 5.3% in 2022/23, up from 3.5% in 2018/19.

6. LWBS in Ontario: 4.92% in 2024 (292,695 of 5,950,047 visits). MEI (Emmanuelle B. Faubert), Sept 2025. https://www.iedm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/economic-note-102025.pdf . Opened and confirmed.
   - About half of Ontario LWBS patients were CTAS 3 (51.4%), so most were not CTAS 4/5. MEI is a free-market think tank; data from freedom-of-information requests.
   - National: CIHI (https://www.cihi.ca/en/emergency-department-wait-times-in-canada-insights-from-a-health-system-perspective/emergency-department-waits-reflect-access-to-care-across-the-system, 25 Jun 2026): "In 2024–2025, 7.7% of patients left before seeing a physician."

7. Ontario median ED length of stay: 3 h 54 min, a 26% increase vs 2021. CP24, 23 Nov 2026, on an MEI report of 19 Nov 2026. https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/11/23/these-are-the-gtha-hospitals-with-the-longest-emergency-room-wait-times-according-to-a-new-report/ . Opened and confirmed. Period probably April 2025 to Feb 2026 (not confirmed).

### Conflicts and what is newer (Part B)

- Credit Valley "4 hours and 56 minutes" for 2024 (CP24, 4 Jun 2025, https://www.cp24.com/photos/2025/06/04/these-are-the-gta-hospitals-with-the-longest-wait-times-according-to-a-new-study/) is labelled time to initial assessment, but it is close to the 5:03 length-of-stay figure and far above 2.7 h and 1:55. Probably mislabelled. Do not use it.
- Averages (Ontario Health, 2.0 h) and 90th percentiles (CCPA 4.5 h, target 3.4 h) measure different things. Do not put them side by side without labels.
- Periods differ: 2022/23 (Auditor General), 2024 (MEI LWBS), 2024-25 (CCPA), April 2025 to Feb 2026 (MEI annex), January 2026 (Ontario Health page, read by the Researcher), July 2026 (owner's scorecard, newest).
