# Accessibility — Ontario hospital virtual ER waiting and recall

## 1. What is the Ontario legal minimum, versus the stronger design target?

### Takeaway
As checked on **2026-11-09**, Ontario's published AODA website guidance still specifies **WCAG 2.0 Level AA**, with defined exceptions; specify **WCAG 2.2 AA as the project's stronger design target**, not as Ontario's current legal minimum. [Ontario guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible); [W3C recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

### Cited Findings
- **Covered organizations/content:** the website rule applies to designated public sector organizations and businesses/non-profits with at least 50 employees. Ontario lists hospitals among designated public sector organizations. It covers public websites and applicable web content published after January 1, 2012, controlled directly or through a contractual relationship; content includes forms and web applications. The AA deadline was January 1, 2021. [Website guidance, updated September 21, 2026](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible); [Public-sector guidance, updated February 19, 2026](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations)
- **Media exceptions:** the specified WCAG 2.0 AA obligation excludes **1.2.4 Captions (Live)** and **1.2.5 Audio Description (Prerecorded)**. This does not exempt prerecorded video captions (1.2.2), nor erase other applicable duties. [Ontario guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible)
- **Scope/practicability exceptions:** Ontario's explanation of IASR ss. 14(5)–(6) addresses pre-2012 content, absence of direct/contractual control, and whether compliance is practicable, including commercial software/tools and significant effects on implementation timelines planned/initiated before 2012. Its separate unconvertible-information explanation cites s. 9. These are fact-specific, not blanket vendor or budget waivers; document evidence, alternatives and remediation. Ordinary intranets/extranets are outside this website obligation, unlike Ontario government/Legislative Assembly internal sites. [Ontario exceptions guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/when-public-websites-are-not-accessible); [Website guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible)
- **Broader duties remain:** public-sector organizations must communicate with regard to disability, consult the person about accessible information/support needs, provide requested accessible information in a timely manner and not charge more. Public safety/emergency information is included. Ontario says IASR standards complement, rather than replace, Human Rights Code accommodation obligations. [Public-sector guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations); [Exceptions guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/when-public-websites-are-not-accessible)
- W3C recommends adopting 2.2 even when formal obligations mention earlier versions. Its 2.2 text removes 4.1.1 Parsing and cautions that organizations obligated to meet 2.0/2.1 may need to continue testing/reporting that criterion. [W3C, “Comparison with WCAG 2.1”](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#comparison-with-wcag-2-1)

### Inferences
- **Candidate brief wording:** “Meet applicable Ontario AODA/IASR requirements, including WCAG 2.0 AA for covered public websites/content, subject to statutory exceptions; design and test the complete waiting/recall journey to WCAG 2.2 AA.” Retain an explicit 2.0 compliance mapping rather than treating the newer target as a change in Ontario law. [Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible); [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#comparison-with-wcag-2-1)
- Do not assume that adding patient login automatically establishes an exempt extranet; obtain a hospital-specific scope determination. Regardless, apply the stronger target to authenticated patient screens. [Ontario exceptions guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/when-public-websites-are-not-accessible)

### Gaps
- The current [O. Reg. 191/11 consolidation](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/110191) returned only an e-Laws application shell to text fetching. Section references above are corroborated by Ontario's substantive official guidance, not independently quoted from the consolidation. The regulation prevails over guidance; legal counsel should confirm organization-specific coverage/exemptions.

## 2. What should the interface implement and test?

### Takeaway
Keyboard, screen-reader semantics, labels, text contrast and enlargement are already central to the 2.0 baseline. Reflow, non-text contrast, programmatic status messages and accessible authentication belong to the stronger 2.2 AA target, including criteria introduced in 2.1. [Ontario's criterion list](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible); [W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)

### Cited Findings
- **Existing baseline:** all actions keyboard-operable without traps; logical focus order and visible focus; meaningful headings/labels, associated form instructions/errors, and programmatic names/roles/states (1.3.1, 2.1.1–2, 2.4.3/6/7, 3.3.1–3, 4.1.2). Do not communicate recall or worsening-condition actions through colour, sound or position alone (1.3.3, 1.4.1). [Ontario criterion list](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible)
- **Existing baseline:** text contrast at least **4.5:1**, or **3:1 for qualifying large text**, subject to criterion exceptions; text resizable to **200%** without loss of content/function, except captions/images of text (1.4.3–4). [W3C contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum); [Resize text](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#resize-text)
- **Stronger AA target:** reflow ordinary vertically scrolling content at **320 CSS pixels** without loss or two-dimensional scrolling, with exceptions for genuinely two-dimensional content (1.4.10); required visual identification of controls/states and meaningful graphics at **3:1** against adjacent colours, with exceptions (1.4.11). Keep focused controls from being entirely hidden by author-created sticky panels (2.4.11). [Reflow](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#reflow); [Non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast); [Focus not obscured](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum)
- **Status announcements:** 4.1.3 AA makes displayed status messages programmatically determinable without receiving focus. W3C specifically includes waiting/progress states, recommends suitable status/live-region roles, and warns against an excessively “chatty” experience. [Normative criterion](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#status-messages); [Official implementation explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)
- **Touch and authentication:** 2.5.8 AA requires targets at least **24 × 24 CSS pixels**, subject to spacing/equivalent-control/inline/user-agent/essential exceptions. **44 × 44** is the enhanced AAA target, not the AA minimum. Under 3.3.8 AA, cognitive authentication tests need an alternative or assistance mechanism, or must meet the criterion's object-recognition/personal-content exceptions; password-manager and copy/paste support are explicit examples. [Minimum targets](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-minimum); [Enhanced targets](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#target-size-enhanced); [Authentication](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#accessible-authentication-minimum)
- **Language/readability:** page/part language identification is A/AA (3.1.1–2); the specific reading-level criterion is AAA (3.1.5). Neither establishes a blanket AA obligation to translate all content into multiple languages. [W3C readable criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#readable)

### Inferences
- **Candidate brief wording:** “Provide clear labelled controls, full keyboard and screen-reader access, visible unobscured focus, adequate contrast, 200% text enlargement and mobile reflow. Announce meaningful waiting/recall changes without moving focus or repeatedly reading countdowns; reserve assertive alerts for genuinely urgent messages. Use generous touch targets, preferably 44 × 44 CSS pixels for primary actions.” These are implementation recommendations grounded in the criteria above, not additional Ontario statutory thresholds. [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [Status guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)
- Provide a labelled text link/manual access route and staff assistance beside any QR code; never make scanning or camera use the only entry/authentication route. Permit password managers/pasting codes and avoid unaided memory/transcription puzzles. [Non-text alternatives](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-content); [Keyboard access](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#keyboard); [Authentication](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#accessible-authentication-minimum)
- Use short, plain-language, clinician-approved safety instructions and professionally reviewed translations for the service population, with interpreter/communication support. These are recommended safety/inclusion measures, not a claim that WCAG AA alone mandates multilingual translation. Test with keyboard, screen readers, zoom/mobile views and people with disabilities; automated scans alone are insufficient. [W3C readable criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#readable); [Ontario testing guidance](https://www.ontario.ca/page/how-make-websites-accessible)

### Gaps
- Hospital-specific French-language-service duties, approved translations, clinical wording and interpreter arrangements require separate verification; no claim about those laws is made here.

## 3. How should equivalent service, response timing and notifications work?

### Takeaway
Make the digital journey optional in service delivery: provide a staff-supported equivalent and do not let a missed browser alert or short countdown automatically determine access to care. These operational safeguards are recommendations grounded in accessibility duties and known notification constraints, not a prescribed Ontario recall protocol. [Ontario public-sector duties](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations); [WCAG timing](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable); [MDN notifications](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Notifications_API/Using_the_Notifications_API)

### Cited Findings
- **Timing already matters at Level A:** 2.2.1 requires a content-set time limit to be turn-off-able, adjustable to at least ten times the default, or extendable following a warning with at least 20 seconds to respond and at least ten extensions. Exceptions include essential timing, real-time events where no alternative is possible, and limits longer than 20 hours. This is not a universal ban on clinical deadlines. [W3C 2.2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable)
- **Verified browser/mobile limits:** notifications require user permission; request it through a user gesture. Chrome/Firefox require a secure context for permission requests and disallow requests in cross-origin iframes. The page-level `Notification()` constructor throws on most mobile browsers; MDN directs mobile implementations to service-worker `showNotification()`. A `close` event does **not** prove that a patient closed or read a notification. [MDN, updated September 2, 2026](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Notifications_API/Using_the_Notifications_API)

### Inferences
- **Candidate brief wording:** “Offer an equivalent staff-assisted waiting and recall pathway for patients without a phone, data, usable digital access or sufficient digital skills, and for disability-related needs. Agree and record an accessible recall method; provide printed/large-print safety instructions and staff-delivered explanations as appropriate. Do not automatically discharge, de-prioritize or cancel a patient solely for missing a notification or short timed response; trigger staff follow-up under a clinically approved escalation policy.” Lack of phone/data is an inclusion recommendation, not itself a disability-law classification. [Ontario duties](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations); [Timing criterion](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#timing-adjustable)
- **Candidate notification wording:** “Notifications are optional reminders, not the sole recall channel or proof of receipt. Show whether alerts are enabled, declined or unsupported, retain the recall message in the interface, and arrange a usable fallback with staff.” Do not promise universal phone/browser support or guaranteed delivery. A telephone-only fallback is not equivalent for everyone; allow in-person/staff communication or suitable communication supports. [MDN limitations](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Notifications_API/Using_the_Notifications_API); [Ontario accessible communication duties](https://www.ontario.ca/page/accessibility-rules-public-sector-organizations)

### Gaps
- No exhaustive compatibility review or iOS-specific installation claim is made. Before enabling notifications, test the actual target browser/OS and implementation, including permission denial, background/locked-device behaviour and loss of connectivity. The reviewed sources do not establish a safe universal recall deadline or escalation procedure; those need hospital clinical/operational approval.
