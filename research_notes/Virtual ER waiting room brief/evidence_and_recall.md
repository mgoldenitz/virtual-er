# Virtual ED waiting rooms and patient recall — Ontario-focused evidence notes

## 1. What intervention is supported, and what did the studies actually show?

### Takeaway
The relevant intervention is clinician-authorized waiting outside the ED waiting room **after in-person triage**, with recall for in-person care. Evidence supports acceptability and freedom to wait elsewhere; it does not establish that waiting at home is universally safe or that recall technology reduces total waiting time or ED crowding. [Ehrler et al.](https://medinform.jmir.org/2022/9/e34488); [Scolnik et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/)

### Cited Findings
- **Review cutoff: 2026-11-09. Four core primary sources:** Ehrler et al., published **2022-09-06**, DOI **10.2196/34488**, publisher URL https://medinform.jmir.org/2022/9/e34488 and full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/; Scolnik et al., **September 2003**, DOI **10.1093/pch/8.7.422**, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/; Ontario Health, undated live **Triage Reassessment** help page, https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm; Sault Area Hospital, undated live **Virtual Home Waiting Room** page, https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/. Live pages were retrieved for this review; their visible content does not establish an effective date or a historical version.
- **Ehrler: single-centre, descriptive cross-sectional mixed-methods evaluation**, Geneva University Hospitals' adult ED outpatient unit; convenience sample of **100 patient questionnaires and 10 nurse questionnaires**, plus utilization logs **October 2017–August 2019**. Patients were at least 16, French-speaking, Swiss triage level 3 or 4; exclusions included impaired discernment, vision/language barriers, severe pain and overly aggressive behaviour. These are study criteria, not a validated Ontario eligibility checklist. [Full text, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- **Outcomes:** **97/100** were satisfied with the recall service; Table 2 shows **75 totally + 22 partly** satisfied. Satisfaction with **waiting time itself was only 56/100** (28 totally + 28 partly). **92/100** reported enough time to return, **86/100** returned on foot, and only **2/100** reported waiting at home. Most waited elsewhere within the hospital. The reported mean wait was approximately **92 minutes**, but there was no concurrent comparison establishing a reduction. [Tables 2 and 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- **Limits:** no strict control group; selected volunteers already willing to use the service; refusal/acceptance denominator not recorded; unvalidated patient questionnaire; few nurse respondents; mainly nearby hospital waiting rather than home waiting. Clinical adverse events, infections and crowding reduction were not comparative outcomes. Thus neither safety equivalence nor shorter total waits nor reduced crowding/infections is demonstrated. Initial staff adoption was constrained by perceived extra workload. Three authors disclosed intellectual-property interests. [Limitations, Discussion and disclosures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- **Internal reporting cautions:** the paper dates its patient survey **2017-03-13 to 2017-04-28**, yet describes adult deployment in **September 2017** and logs starting in October. It reports 10 nurse respondents, with a denominator of 25 in one section but a 20% response rate elsewhere. Location is 79% inside the hospital in prose versus 80% in Table 2; the prose also confusingly labels 75% as satisfied with waiting time, whereas Table 2 clearly assigns 75% to being totally satisfied with the service. Prefer the tabulated service/wait-time distinction and say “most stayed nearby”; do not silently repair dates or calculate a nurse response rate. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- **Ontario clinical precedent:** Scolnik et al. at Toronto's Hospital for Sick Children was a controlled, single-blind trial **randomized by study day**, not by individual. **61 families** were enrolled: no intervention 23, colouring set 18, pager 20. Families had already undergone triage and waited at least one hour; emergent patients, certain infectious presentations and non-English-speaking families were excluded. Pagers mainly enabled movement around the hospital, not validated home waiting. [Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/)
- Parents liked pagers; more pager families left the waiting room (**16/20 versus 19/40**, P=.016, as reported), but there was **no statistically significant improvement in overall satisfaction** or difference in recorded waiting-time measures. One pager patient left before seeing a doctor. Small sample, two summer months, English-speaking families, historical workflow and satisfaction-focused outcomes limit generalization; this was not a clinical safety/noninferiority trial. [Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/)

### Inferences
- The defensible product rationale is **patient choice and waiting experience**, with potential physical waiting-room benefits to be tested—not a demonstrated solution to hospital access block or ED-wide crowding. Home waiting requires additional evaluation because these studies largely involved people remaining nearby. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [Scolnik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/)

### Gaps
- These sources do not quantify deterioration, missed-recall harm, safe travel distances, or comparative adverse-event rates for post-triage home waiting. This focused review is not an exhaustive systematic review and cannot establish absence of newer evidence.

## 2. How does Ontario context differ, and which timings are actually supported?

### Takeaway
Do not conflate a post-triage recall workflow with a virtual clinical consultation or pre-arrival queue registration. No universal **10/20/30-minute recall ladder** was identified; triage reassessment intervals and administrative message intervals serve different purposes. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [Ontario Health eCTAS](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm); [Sault Area Hospital](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)

### Cited Findings
- **Post-triage recall:** Ehrler's nurse enrolls an already-triaged patient; an administrative clerk verifies the telephone number; the nurse estimates the wait and validates registration; a confirmation message precedes leaving. Triage nurse discretion controls the first recall. The queue advances by arrival time and emergency level, whether the patient is physically present or not. Swiss four-level triage is not interchangeable with Canadian five-level CTAS. [System description and setting](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- **Sault Area Hospital's VHWR is pre-arrival coordination:** patients submit an online form for listed non-urgent complaints, receive texts, and are **triaged and registered after arriving**. Its terms say it does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment and manages ED arrival times. This is neither an already-completed in-person triage pathway nor a virtual urgent-care clinician consultation. The page's claimed benefits are not a controlled evaluation of post-triage home waiting. [Official hospital page](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)
- SAH verifies SMS access with a code and confirms enrollment. It sends queue updates every **60 minutes**; an offer to attend requires a response, with reminders every **30 minutes**, described as up to five messages over **2.5 hours**, then removal for no response. After responding, patients receive arrival instructions and should come as soon as possible, allowing for travel/children. No texts: access a community clinic or proceed to the ED. Worsening: go directly to the nearest ED or call 911 rather than wait for a message. These are **this hospital's pre-arrival operating rules**, not universal post-triage standards. [FAQ and consent](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)
- **Ontario Health eCTAS recommended clinical reassessment:** CTAS 1 continuous monitoring; CTAS 2 every **15 minutes**; CTAS 3 every **30**; CTAS 4 every **60**; CTAS 5 every **120**. Nurses document reassessment/no change and vitals; overdue clocks flag delay. These are reassessment recommendations, **not permission to leave**, a callback ladder, a guarantee of physician attendance, or a substitute for assessment when symptoms change. [Official eCTAS guidance](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm)
- Ehrler's UI turns orange **20 minutes before expected return**, suggesting but not mandating recall; first recall is nurse-controlled. If the patient has not arrived after **20 minutes**, reminders repeat every 20 minutes. The paper describes a four-SMS total/final warning that queue position is no longer guaranteed **but the visit remains possible**; its wording about reminder/message counts is somewhat ambiguous, so avoid deriving an exact terminal elapsed time. It does not validate automatic discharge/removal. [System description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)
- Ehrler's **Table 3 entries at 10, 20 and 30 minutes are patient interest thresholds for choosing the service**, not tested escalation timings. Neither that study nor the Ontario sources validate a universal 10/20/30 ladder. [Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/)

### Inferences
- Set any prototype reminder ladder as **configurable demonstration policy**, not “CTAS-compliant” or clinically validated. Separate the reassessment clock, estimated return time, recall acknowledgement deadline and expected arrival deadline. Their ownership and clinically acceptable values would require a local hospital protocol. [Ontario eCTAS](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm); [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)

### Gaps
- No province-wide post-triage off-site waiting eligibility or missed-callback/removal protocol was established from the retrieved sources. SAH's public rules do not describe a patient who acknowledges “on my way” but never arrives, or a staff review requirement before its pre-arrival removal. Do not generalize its automatic expiry to patients already triaged in an ED.

## 3. What should the synthetic prototype demonstrate, and how should the brief summarize it?

### Takeaway
The following are **risk-informed design recommendations, not validated clinical requirements**. For this assignment the prototype is limited to synthetic patients, browser alerts, and no hospital integration or real patient contacts; it must not suggest that an alert delivers care or establishes safety.

### Cited Findings
- Clinician involvement before enrollment, telephone verification, confirmation, nurse-controlled initial recall and explicit handling of non-arrival are present in Ehrler's implementation; acknowledgement and contact-failure fallback appear in SAH's different pre-arrival service; Ontario eCTAS separately documents clinical reassessment. These provide design precedents, not validation of a complete combined workflow. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/); [Ontario eCTAS](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm)

### Inferences
#### Recommended demonstration safeguards — not a deployable hospital protocol
- **Eligibility:** require a simulated authorized clinician decision after simulated in-person triage; record permission, review time, ability to return, communication/accessibility needs and voluntary choice. A low CTAS number/category alone must not trigger authorization. This recommendation extrapolates from clinician selection in the studies. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [Scolnik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/)
- **Reassessment/deterioration:** retain an independently visible reassessment-due state and staff responsibility; deterioration flags demand staff review, not a routine queue message. Explain that symptoms worsening should not wait for an alert; real implementation would require clinician-approved return/emergency instructions and a feasible reassessment arrangement. A browser check-in is not a clinical reassessment. [Ontario eCTAS](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)
- **Contact and acknowledgement:** simulate contact verification and an initial test alert before authorization; distinguish **alert created / delivery unknown / acknowledged / on the way / physically arrived**. Never infer receipt from sending, or attendance from acknowledgement. Keep all contact fields fictitious and do not implement real messaging. Phone verification and SAH's explicit offer-response step are precedents; the proposed state model is a design recommendation. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)
- **Missed callback and acknowledged-but-no-arrival:** separate these exceptions, show an overdue alert to the simulated staff user, retain the patient on an exception list, and log attempts/decisions. Require an explicit authorized staff decision and reason before removal/closure; never silently auto-discharge because a demo timer expired. This is a conservative recommendation, **not an Ontario rule established by these sources**; Ehrler preserves the possibility of a visit after repeated reminders, while SAH's different pre-arrival service expires unanswered offers. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)
- **Contact failure/downtime:** demonstrate failure states, pause new off-site enrollment, preserve outstanding recalls and route to manual staff review/reconciliation. In a real service, verified alternative contact or supervised on-site waiting would need a hospital-approved fallback; a student browser-only demo can simulate these actions but cannot supply them. SAH's published fallback for missing texts is to seek care by another route, not continue relying on the queue. [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)

#### Suggested two-paragraph synthesis
Virtual ED waiting should be defined narrowly: selected patients receive in-person clinical triage, are explicitly authorized to wait elsewhere, and are recalled for in-person assessment. It is not a virtual urgent-care consultation or simply reserving a place before arrival; Sault Area Hospital's VHWR is an example of the latter, with triage on arrival. In Ehrler et al.'s 2022 Geneva study, 97 of 100 surveyed users were satisfied with an SMS recall service, but most stayed within the hospital and only two waited at home. The uncontrolled, convenience-sample study assessed experience and adoption, not comparative safety, crowding reduction or shorter total waits. A small Toronto paediatric pager trial similarly found favourable reactions without a significant improvement in overall satisfaction. [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [Scolnik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2791651/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)

An Ontario-oriented prototype should therefore demonstrate clinician-controlled eligibility, an explicit reassessment/deterioration pathway, simulated contact verification, recall acknowledgement, and separate staff-reviewed exceptions for no response and “on the way” without arrival. It should show a downtime/contact-failure fallback and require an explicit staff decision before closing an unresolved post-triage record; these are design recommendations, not validated clinical rules. Ontario eCTAS reassessment recommendations are acuity-dependent and are not a recall ladder. Neither those recommendations nor the study establishes universal 10/20/30-minute escalation. With synthetic data, browser alerts only, and no hospital integration or real contacts, the student project demonstrates workflow states—not remote clinical monitoring or a service safe for real patients. [Ontario eCTAS](https://help.accesstocare.on.ca/helpfiles/eCTASOnlineHelp/Triage_Reassessment/Triage_Reassessment.htm); [Ehrler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9490523/); [SAH](https://sah.on.ca/programs-services/emergency-services/virtual-home-waiting-room/)

### Gaps
- The retrieved evidence does not validate the recommended exception states, manual closure safeguard, universal time thresholds, downtime plan, or clinical eligibility algorithm. No operational/readiness claim should be inferred from the synthetic prototype; clinical, accessibility, privacy, integration and reliability validation remain outside this research assignment.
