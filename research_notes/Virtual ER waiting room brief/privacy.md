# Ontario privacy: virtual ER waiting and recall

Research date: 2026-11-09. Actionable research, not legal advice. **Statute** below means a legislative duty; **IPC guidance** describes regulator expectations; **design recommendation** is an application to this proposed system, not a prescribed technical specification.

## What information is protected, and who is accountable?

### Takeaway
A name-free queue is not necessarily de-identified. For a hospital deployment, design around the hospital's PHIPA accountability and authorized agents, not a claim that using ticket codes removes privacy obligations. [PHIPA, ss. 2–4, 17](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC virtual-care guidance, pp. 2–3](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Cited Findings
- **Statute:** PHIPA s. 4(1)(b) includes identifying information relating to provision of health care; s. 4(2) includes reasonably foreseeable identification using the information alone **or with other information**. Section 4(3) addresses identifying information in mixed records; s. 2 defines de-identification. Removing direct identifiers is therefore not the complete test. [PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC FAQ, printed p. 6](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf).
- **Statute:** Hospitals are custodians under s. 3(1), para. 4(i). Section 17 governs agents' authority and custodian responsibility; agents act for the custodian, not their own purposes. IPC distinguishes agents from non-agent electronic service providers and from health information network providers (HINPs). A platform is not a HINP merely because multiple hospitals buy it: the latter category concerns services primarily enabling custodians to disclose PHI to one another. [PHIPA, ss. 3, 10(4), 17](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC virtual-care guidance, pp. 2–3](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Statute:** Sections 29–30 require lawful authority for collection/use/disclosure and, subject to statutory exceptions, prohibit using PHI where other information suffices or more PHI than reasonably necessary. IPC recommends minimum-necessary staff access, training and maintained need-to-know authorizations. [PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC guidance, pp. 2, 4, 7](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Design recommendation:** Treat a linked ticket, hospital/site, waiting/recall status and precise timestamps as potentially PHI when staff, companions or another dataset can connect them to a patient. A pseudonymous identifier is not proof of de-identification. Do not publish a patient-level queue or expose lookup by short sequential ticket alone. [Basis: PHIPA s. 4](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03).
- **Design recommendation:** Keep the student demo synthetic: invented visits, simulated notifications, no copied hospital exports or real patient phone numbers. Even a synthetic queue can collect real visitors' IP addresses, device identifiers and access logs; these are not automatically PHI, but may be personal information or become health-linked through context. Minimize analytics and disclose actual collection rather than promising “no personal data.” [Basis: PHIPA s. 4; IPC FAQ, printed pp. 5–6, distinction between PHI and other personal information](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf).

### Gaps
- The hospital, integrations and actual data flows are unspecified; custodian/agent/provider classification and lawful authority need hospital privacy review. [Relevant framework: IPC guidance, pp. 2–3](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

## What communication and access safeguards should the design implement?

### Takeaway
Use a minimal recall notice and put individualized status behind authenticated access. A patient's agreement to texting does not replace reasonable safeguards or eliminate shared-phone risks. [PHIPA, ss. 12, 30](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC guidance, pp. 6–9, 11](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Cited Findings
- **Statute:** Section 12(1) requires reasonable protection against theft, loss, unauthorized use/disclosure, copying, modification and disposal. **IPC guidance:** organization-approved systems, strong passwords, identity verification, privacy-protective defaults, access controls and maintained/monitored audit logs are expected safeguards; portal access needs initial and subsequent identification/authentication. [PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC guidance, pp. 4–8, 11](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **IPC guidance:** explain virtual-care risks and document the discussion/consent; verify recipients; minimize message contents; prefer encrypted communication, assessing reasonableness if encryption is infeasible. Its detailed messaging discussion concerns email/secure messaging, **not a blanket approval of ordinary SMS**. [IPC guidance, pp. 6, 8–9](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Design recommendation:** Confirm the phone number, whether it is shared and what sender/content is safe; record the patient's channel preference and provide a non-SMS alternative. Use a hospital-approved neutral template, e.g. “Please return to the agreed check-in point now. Follow the instructions provided by your care team. For help call [verified number].” Explain the sender in advance. Omit name, symptoms, triage category, diagnosis and ticket secret. Even a neutral notice or recognizable hospital sender can reveal attendance; it is not anonymous. [Basis: IPC guidance, pp. 6, 8–9](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Design recommendation:** Separate a visible queue label from a high-entropy, cryptographically random patient access secret. Scope access to one visit; expire/revoke it; rate-limit failed lookups and recall requests; prevent enumeration; use generic errors and short inactive sessions. Do not use date of birth, phone number or a short ticket as the sole secret. These are proposed controls, not PHIPA-mandated token lengths or algorithms. [Basis: PHIPA s. 12](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC guidance, pp. 7, 11](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Design recommendation:** Use individual staff accounts, strong authentication/MFA, server-side role/site authorization and least privilege; restrict recall/status changes to assigned roles. Audit access, status changes, recalls and administrative actions with actor/time/outcome; protect and review logs. Keep raw secrets, full SMS bodies and unnecessary PHI out of application/error logs. [Basis: IPC guidance, pp. 4, 7–8](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Gaps
- Do not state that CASL blanket-bans health service texts. CRTC's FAQ distinguishes commercial electronic messages from non-commercial messages and includes commercial SMS; exact wording, purpose and any promotional additions need assessment. The CRTC page was found in official search results but direct retrieval returned HTTP 403, so this is a limited verification, not a healthcare-specific CASL ruling. [CRTC FAQ, “What is a commercial electronic message?” and SMS question](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm).
- The indexed 2026 e-Laws text marks the general s. 10.1 electronic-audit-log amendment as **not yet in force**. Do not cite it as an operative universal duty without rechecking commencement. Logging remains important under IPC safeguard guidance; HINPs have additional specific logging duties described there. [PHIPA, s. 10.1 editorial note](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03?search=electronic+audit+log); [IPC guidance, pp. 3–4, 7](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

## What must happen before real deployment, and what should the brief say?

### Takeaway
Real hospital use needs hospital privacy/security approval, assessed vendors, enforceable safeguards and a hospital-approved retention schedule. Neither “Canada-only hosting” nor “delete every record after 24 hours” is a universal PHIPA rule. [IPC FAQ, printed pp. 13, 31](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf); [IPC guidance, pp. 4–5, 9–10](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Cited Findings
- **IPC guidance:** conduct a privacy impact assessment; assess threats/security risks; implement policy, training and a breach-management protocol. Vendor agreements should address prompt breach notification, security audits, need-to-know access and secure return/destruction at contract end. HINPs have additional specified requirements, including PIAs/TRAs and written agreements; do not generalize that special category's statutory duties to every vendor. [IPC guidance, pp. 3–5](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Hosting:** IPC expressly states PHIPA does not require PHI to be retained/stored in Ontario or Canada; outsourcing does not remove custodian accountability or safeguards. This is not unrestricted permission: assess actual storage, backups, remote support, subprocessors, foreign-law exposure and hospital contractual/policy requirements. [IPC FAQ, printed p. 31](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf); [IPC vendor guidance, p. 5](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Retention statute/guidance:** PHIPA s. 13 requires secure retention/transfer/disposal and preservation for pending access-request recourse. IPC says PHIPA does not establish specific general retention periods; governing legislation and institutional requirements matter. Virtual interactions retain ordinary record-retention/access/correction requirements; unnecessary duplicate message copies can be removed when appropriately documented in the patient's record. [PHIPA, s. 13](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC FAQ, printed p. 13](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf); [IPC guidance, pp. 9–10](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Design recommendation:** Before production, map the hospital, application/cloud/SMS/logging vendors and subprocessors; assess a PIA and threat model; encrypt transport and stored data/backups with controlled keys; prohibit unrelated vendor analytics/advertising/model training; agree access, incident response, data location, audit and exit/deletion terms. Establish separate approved schedules for transient queue state, clinical documentation, delivery records, audit logs and backups, subject to legal holds. A short token expiry is not permission to delete the clinical record. [Basis: IPC guidance, pp. 4–5, 7, 9–10](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf).
- **Recommended brief wording:** “This student prototype uses synthetic visits and simulated recalls, not real patient information. A hospital deployment would treat linked visit codes, site, status and timestamps as potentially identifying health information; minimize collection and message content; authenticate patient access and restrict staff actions by role; and use reviewed safeguards, vendors and audit logging. Real use requires hospital privacy/security review and hospital-approved retention and breach-response policies. Hosting location and retention would be assessed for that deployment—not assumed to be a universal Canada-only or 24-hour-deletion PHIPA requirement.” [Basis: PHIPA, ss. 4, 12, 17, 30](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03); [IPC guidance](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2021/02/virtual-health-care-visits.pdf); [IPC FAQ, printed pp. 13, 31](https://www.ipc.on.ca/sites/default/files/legacy/2015/11/phipa-faq.pdf).
